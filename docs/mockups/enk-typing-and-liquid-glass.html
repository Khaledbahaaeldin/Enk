Enk typing and liquid glass

Two mockups for the next spec. Part 1 is the typewriter strike: what happens on every key press. Part 2 is the liquid glass component set with real refraction (a browser can do it with SVG displacement maps; the app will use Backdrop's lens). Both are interactive.

1. Typing: the strike

Parts of the strike
Try
  • Type on your keyboard or the keys on the phone. Enter returns the carriage and Backspace wipes a letter.
  • Strike is the proposal: the slug swings up and hits the paper at 26 ms, the letter appears there at full ink (no fade), the paper thumps, the carriage ticks one column. The sound plays on touch-down, so the audio output delay lands on the impact.
  • Type fast. Strikes overlap and the extras scale back, so it keeps up.
  • Open a note shows the type-on: the carriage sweeps each line and the letters appear as it passes, instead of a fade.

Times in the spec are starting values for a phone. The sound here is a small synthesised click, only to show the timing.

2. Liquid glass components

Effects level
  • Drag the glass puck over the headline and the stripes: the rim bends the picture behind it.
  • Nav bar: tap or drag the lens. It stretches with speed and a flick carries it.
  • Toggle, slider, segmented: the moving part is a lens that grows on touch and refracts what sits under it.
  • Button: press and it swells. Menu: tap "Options" and the button itself grows into the menu (one piece of glass changes shape).
  • Switch to Balanced (blur and tint, no bending) and Light (solid) to see what the lower levels keep.

This page needs a Chromium-based browser for the refraction (it uses an SVG filter inside backdrop-filter). In the app the same look is drawn by Backdrop's lens on Android 13 and later.
